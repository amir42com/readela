## Observed on the real site

Read on 2026-10-12 in the owner's signed-in Chrome, in a tab the owner does
not see (the page reports itself hidden), structure only.

- **Why the newest reply lost Paper or Night.** ChatGPT puts parts the
  reader can edit in place inside a reply: a block marked
  `data-oai-writing-block-surface`, with rounded corners and no background
  of its own, that holds an element with `contenteditable="true"` and
  `role="textbox"`, and in it headings, paragraphs and lists. In a plain
  reply it is a direct child of the element that holds the reply's text; in
  a reply built of components it sits in the component column. Up to
  `247366f` a response that held somewhere to type was not read at all, on
  the rule that Readela never touches what the reader writes. The whole
  reply therefore stayed as the site made it, whichever theme was chosen:
  the regression the owner saw. A reply is first rendered without the
  editable part being editable and is made so a moment later, which is why
  the colour could show briefly after a reload and then go.
- **The marks a turn carries.** `data-turn-key` is a long random identifier,
  different for every turn, and every turn has a parent of its own. Nothing
  seen there would let one turn be taken for another.
- **Long conversations could not be examined.** In a hidden tab the site
  does not load earlier turns when the page is scrolled, so what the site
  does on the way to a place far above was not seen. See "Not verified".
- **Auto direction.** Of 285 blocks in five conversations, the only blocks
  with both scripts that Auto read as left-to-right were headings of one
  Persian word and two Latin ones. The sentences the owner described — a
  Persian sentence with many Latin terms — were not among them and were
  built by hand for the tests.

## What was corrected

Commit `e43776f`.

### Reading text and the site's own parts

- **One decision.** Which parts of a reply are reading text and which are
  the site's own was decided in several places, with slightly different
  rules. It is now decided in one (`partsOf` in `src/page/reader.js`), from
  the structure alone: a direct part of the surface is reading text, a part
  of the flow, a wrapper to look into, or a unit that stays the site's.
  Marking, the theme, the font, the size and the bookmark all follow that
  one answer.
- **A reply with a part to type in** is read like any other. The part to
  type in is a unit: it stays the site's, whole, and nothing in it is read,
  marked or written. Everything else in the reply takes the theme. When a
  part becomes editable after it was rendered, the reply is looked at again
  (the observer now also hears `contenteditable` change).
- **A unit Readela has never seen** — one the site adapter names nowhere —
  is found by the same rule and carries `data-readela-unit="found"`. It
  keeps the site's direction, font and size under every setting, and a
  paragraph inside it is no place for a bookmark.
- **The first frame.** A unit used to be measured a moment after it
  arrived, and until then it could show the theme's colours under the
  site's text. It now gets the site's background and text colour as it is
  marked, in the same task as the arrival, and is corrected to what it
  needs once measured.
- **A change of the system's colour scheme** called the measuring function
  with the browser's event as its argument, which the function took for an
  element to measure, and failed. It is now called without one.

### Bookmarks

- **One writer.** A save, an update or a removal read the stored places,
  changed them and wrote them back, in the tab where it happened. The
  browser's storage has no transactions, so two tabs doing this at the same
  moment each wrote their own result and one change was lost; the unit test
  "two changes that run at the same moment, each on its own, lose one of
  them" shows it. Every change is now made by the background component,
  one after another (`createMarkWriter` in `src/browser/storage.js`); the
  page asks it with a message and never writes. The background component
  answers only the extension's own pages and content scripts, accepts three
  requests — save a place, remove a place, note the sidebar row of an
  existing place — and checks each as before. It keeps no state: the one
  variable it holds is the end of the queue, and when the browser stops and
  restarts it a new queue begins on what is stored. A change that is not
  answered within five seconds is reported to the reader as failed.
- **Return.** In a long conversation the site can move the page again a
  moment after a place has been brought into view. Return now looks a
  second time after the page has settled; a place that has moved is put
  back, at most six times, and Return says "arrived" only for a place that
  stayed. One that will not stay is reported as not reached.

### Auto direction

A block is right-to-left when at least 40% of its words are. Below that
share, Auto used to say left-to-right, which is wrong for a Persian sentence
that names many Latin terms. Below the share it now looks at the shape of
the sentence: where it begins, where it ends, and in how many separate
places its right-to-left words stand. A sentence that begins right-to-left
and either ends so, or has right-to-left words in two or more places, or has
at least 20% of them, is right-to-left; one that begins left-to-right is
right-to-left only when it ends right-to-left with such words in two or
more places. The direction of the text around settles what the shape leaves
open. A list is decided by its items, not by the sum of their words. An
English sentence that quotes a Persian word, numbers, code, web addresses
and mathematics are read as before.

## Typography

### What was compared

Readela Sans as it stood drew Arabic script with Vazirmatn (the Non-Latin
build) and Latin with Inter, each declared for the characters of its own
script. The baseline found that the space between two Persian words was
Inter's, so a Persian sentence was shaped in dozens of pieces, and that the
heap of the page grew under that load and did not come back.

Candidates were taken from their publishers' own releases, kept outside the
repository with archives, licences, versions and digests, and inspected file
by file (`REPORT.md` and `manifest.json` in the evidence folder). Nothing
was installed and no downloaded file was run. Seven families were looked at
and not kept at that stage: Readex Pro (36 of 44 Persian letters, no
Persian digits), Rubik and Cairo (42 of 44 letters), Mada (no joiner
characters), Sahel (no accented Latin, and no Latin at all in its variable
file), and Shabnam and Samim (discontinued by their author; not downloaded).

long Persian, long English, Persian with Latin terms, English with Persian

### Seen: reading quality

- **A and B** look the same. In B the space of a Persian sentence is 4.30 px
  wide at 16 px instead of 4.45 px, and its full stop, colon, parentheses,
  hyphen, slash, brackets, guillemets and dashes are Vazirmatn's.
- **Inter** is the only Latin among the candidates with an italic of its
  own; every one-family candidate slants its upright.
- **C, Vazirmatn (full)**: the Persian is the Persian of today, glyph for
  glyph. Its Latin is a Roboto-like design drawn to sit with the Persian: a
  little narrower than Inter and plainer, and the two scripts match in
  weight and height better than in any other configuration. No italic of
  its own.
- **D, Estedad**: a different Persian design; its Latin is narrow with tight
  word spaces (3.13 px), and it was the slowest to lay out.
- **E, Noto Sans Arabic (full)**: the Latin that comes merged into it does
  not get bolder with the weight (the measured width of a Latin sample is
  the same at 400, 500, 600 and 700). Rejected for that.
- **F, IBM Plex Sans Arabic**: four static files, no weight above 700, a
  geometric Persian further from what readers of the product know, and no
  joiner characters in its own table (Chrome shaped them correctly all the
  same).

D, E and F were not taken further. B2 was not taken further either: it
shows Latin terms inside Persian text in Vazirmatn's Latin and English
paragraphs in Inter, two Latin designs on one page.

### What was chosen

Commit `9a76f6b` puts **B+K** into the product: Vazirmatn declared a second
time for right-to-left text with the space and the marks of a sentence, the
stylesheet choosing by the direction Readela gave the nearest block, and
the two Inter faces declared without kerning. The font files are the same
two, unmodified, and the popup still offers Original and Readela Sans and
nothing else. The built stylesheet differs from the measured B+K build in
one rule: a web address kept left-to-right as a unit inside a Persian
paragraph stays whole in the Latin font.

What this changes for a reader, to be judged on the real sites: the space
in Persian text is 3% narrower; the marks of a Persian sentence are
Vazirmatn's; English is set without Inter's kerning, so some lines break at
a different word.

Two limits. With Direction set to Original, Readela gives no block a
direction and the fonts are declared as before, so a Persian sentence is
again shaped in pieces; Auto, the default, and RTL are not affected. And a
site that sets `font-kerning: normal` on its text gets Inter's kerning
back, with its cost; whether ChatGPT or Claude do was not read.

**C is the replacement to consider, and it is the owner's to decide.** It
is not in the product: DEC-142 names Inter with Vazirmatn. It would make
the package 677 KB smaller, start about 9 MiB lower in memory, and give up
Inter's Latin and its true italic. An experimental build of the final code
with Vazirmatn alone is kept beside the test builds for that comparison,
named as experimental in its manifest. It was loaded once in Chrome on the
benchmark page: the one face is used and the bookmark actions answer.

DEC-143 is the owner's to say; this work does not call the build ready for
release.

took between a third and a half of one processor core during the cases
(most of it the desktop program this work was driven from; during the
first case also a file-synchronisation program), and each result lists
them. The machine was asked to stay awake and did. Memory is that of one
process read from the browser's own dumps and from the system; it does not
depend on what else runs, as processor time does.

## Privacy and trust effect

- The background component now writes to the extension's own storage. It
  did not before; it only passed commands on. It reads and writes the
  stored places and nothing else, on a request from the extension itself
  (`sender.id` is checked), and makes no network request. No permission was
  added: `storage` was already declared.
- What is stored is unchanged: the same record, the same limit of 1,000
  places, the same refusals.
- A part of a reply the reader can type in is never read: it is a unit, and
  units are not walked.
- No font is fetched from anywhere but the extension's own package.
