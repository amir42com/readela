# Measurement evidence — Readela 0.3.0, controlled memory and processor baseline (2026-10-12)

Work unit: ENT-246 under DEC-144 and DEC-143, with the light assertion
`docs/project-preflight/ent-246-readela-0.3.0-performance-baseline.json`.

This file records what was measured on 2026-10-12 on the unchanged code of
the fourth correction (`247366f`, code `0904083`), before any further
correction, and how. It is dated evidence, not a statement of current
status. No product code was changed by this work, nothing was pushed, merged,
tagged, submitted or published, and Personal OS was not written to.

[2026-10-11-readela-0.3.0-correction-4.md](2026-10-11-readela-0.3.0-correction-4.md)
is left as it was written. Three of its statements about memory are narrowed
here by measurement; see "What the earlier evidence had wrong".

## Kinds of statement in this file

- **Measured** — numbers from the run, with the method.
- **Verified** — run and observed in this work, on synthetic pages.
- **Inferred** or **not established** — marked as such.

Memory is in MiB (1,048,576 bytes). "Private" is the private bytes of a
process as Windows reports them; "working set" is what of it is resident.

## What was corrected in the measuring script

`test/perf/run.mjs`, commit `37e53fe`. The page (`test/perf/fixtures/page.html`)
is unchanged.

- **Matched actions.** A browser without the extension now does in the page
  what the script does there for a bookmark and a return: the same paragraph
  scrolled to and selected twice a cycle, and on every other cycle scrolled
  smoothly to where a return puts it. It also has a second, empty page where
  the other cases have the popup.
- **One case at a time**, each in a browser and a profile of its own, never
  side by side.
- **A fixed number of cycles at a fixed pace** (150 in a block, 6.4 s each),
  not a fixed time.
- **A warm boundary.** Ten cycles, a check that no font is still loading, and
  a minute at rest before the first block.
- **Nothing collected by force** from the first cycle to the end of the last
  rest. The processes are read from the operating system every five seconds
  by one helper that asks nothing of the browser; memory dumps at the ends of
  the blocks show the heaps as the work left them. A collection is forced
  only at the very end, and then the browser is told that memory is short.
- **Memory dumps checked and kept whole.** A dump that lacks the page's
  process or its collected heap gives no numbers (before, zeros). The dump
  tells apart what the browser's collected heap has taken, what is allocated
  in it, and what lies free on its lists. All 28 dumps of the run are valid.
- **Identity in the result**: browser version, a digest of the build, the
  digests of the script and the page, the commit, the options, the order, and
  what every other program on the machine used meanwhile.

The cycle of the long run (`soak`) is shared with the new suite and is
unchanged; its two cases remain an unmatched pair and are labelled so.

## Method

Cases, in the order they ran: **control** (no extension, matched actions),
**Night** (Readela, Night, the site's typography), **Night + Readela Sans**,
**defaults** (Readela as installed). Twenty stored bookmarks in the three
cases with the extension, as in the long run.

Each case: ten warm cycles; one minute at rest; **block 1**, 150 cycles; five
minutes left alone; **block 2**, the same 150 cycles; five minutes left
alone; then a forced collection, then the memory-pressure signal. A cycle is
the cycle of the long run without changes of settings: another conversation
shown, a reply streamed for 2.5 s, sidebar rows replaced, the conversation
scrolled, a bookmark saved and updated, returned to on every other cycle,
and removed.

The work of a cycle took 5.67 to 5.76 s (middle value) of the 6.4 s in every
case, 5.93 s at most; no cycle ran over. The extension answered `saved` 600
times, `arrived` 150 times and removed 300 times in each of its cases. A save
took 7.3 ms (the control's step 1.3 ms), a return 280 to 287 ms (the
control's 273 ms), a removal 3.4 ms.

## Isolation actually achieved

**Measured**, per case of 44.5 minutes:

- The whole machine was 4.7 to 5.0 % busy (middle value over 515 readings;
  95th percentile 6.2 to 7.1 %; never above 14.1 %): about one of twenty
  logical processors, the measured browser included.
- Other programs whose use the system lets be read used 1,173 to 1,253
  processor-seconds (0.44 to 0.47 of one processor). Of that, the desktop
  application this work was directed from used 811 to 860 s; two
  input-device helpers 191 to 201 s; the ChatGPT application 53 to 64 s; the
  owner's Chrome (15 processes, left running) 6 to 7 s; Notion, Steam, a
  password manager and the waiting shell 9 to 29 s each.
- No other build, download, browser test or measurement ran. About 12 GiB of
  memory was free.

It was a lightly loaded machine, not an idle one, and the load was the same
for the four cases.

## Results: what is on the pages that stay

**Measured**, from the dumps after the forced collection.

- Under Readela Sans the same document is laid out with **9,658 shaping
  runs where the other three cases have 2,527**.
- Under Readela Sans the browser's performance timeline holds **200
  long-animation-frame records** (its limit; 10 after the warm cycles, 151
  after block 1), where the other cases hold 1 to 4. A record is made when a
  frame takes longer than 50 ms. Layout on the main thread took 5.6 s in a
  block under Night + Readela Sans against 4.5 s under Night.
- Of the 335 pages of ordinary objects left under Readela Sans, 120 (15 MiB)
  hold one of those records, and on 42 it is the only thing on the page;
  32 (4 MiB) hold nothing but the table records. In the other cases 1 to 3
  pages hold such a record.
- The records are not the whole of it: block 2 added 70 pages for 50 new
  records, block 1 89 pages for 141.

## The probe: why there are more runs

**Verified**, on a synthetic page without the extension
(`performance/analysis/probe-font-runs.mjs`; 200 paragraphs of one sentence,
runs counted in a memory dump after a collection):

| Text | The site's font | Readela Sans as declared | With the Arabic-script face also declared for the space |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Persian, 19 words | 1 run a paragraph | 37 | 1 |
| English, 19 words | 1 | 1 | 37 |
| Persian with Latin terms, 23 words | 9 | 42 | 16 |

The Arabic-script face is declared for the characters of its script only,
and the space is not one of them. Every space between two Persian words is
therefore taken from the Latin face: a Persian sentence becomes one run for
each word and one for each space, and a page of Persian alone loads Inter as
well as Vazirmatn. Declaring the space for the Arabic-script face moves the
same split into English, because that face is named first: which face draws
a space has to follow the text around it, and that is a design question for
the typography, not a one-line change.

## What is established, and what is not

- **Established for this page and this length of run.**
  - The rise all cases share is room the browser's heap works in. The same
    block uses it again, and one collection returns it. It is not retained
    and it is not Readela's.
  - Readela's own logic (defaults) and the Night theme add no growth beyond
    the control's.
  - Readela Sans adds growth of another kind: pages of the browser's
    collected heap that hold almost nothing and are not given back, 11 and
    9 MiB in two blocks of 150 cycles, with no sign of levelling. What is
    held is no more than in the other cases.
  - Nothing Readela holds grows: not its JavaScript, not nodes, not
    listeners, not anything in the collected heap.
  - A page left alone for five minutes returns nothing.
  - Under Readela Sans the text is shaped in 3.8 times as many runs, because
    of how the two faces are declared.
- **Inferred, not tested.** That the many small runs are why the pages
  fragment: more and smaller short-lived objects for every render, more
  frames over 50 ms, and so more long-lived records of the browser's own
  scattered among them. The run did not vary the number of runs with
  everything else held the same.
- **Not established.** Whether the growth under Readela Sans has a bound; the
  earlier hour suggests it goes on at about the rate of block 2. Whether it
  happens on the real sites, whose own frames run over 50 ms with or without
  Readela and whose text is not this text. How much one run differs from the
  next: each case ran once. Whether the Claude-shaped page behaves the same.
- **Not supported by anything measured.** That Inter or Vazirmatn as files
  cost growing memory (1.85 MiB, constant); that Readela keeps a growing
  structure; that the browser returns the memory when the page is idle.

## What the earlier evidence had wrong

- "Nothing that is held grows" stands. "It is free space in pages the
  collected heap keeps" is true of two different things that the earlier
  runs could not tell apart: room in the backings, which is returned, and
  nearly empty pages of ordinary objects, which are not.
- "The browser does the same to the page alone at a higher rate of
  rendering" does not account for Readela Sans: at the same rate, with the
  same actions, the page alone and the page under Night keep 0.1 to 2 MiB of
  such pages in a block where Readela Sans keeps 9 to 11.
- The growth without the extension recorded for the hour (8 and 10 MiB)
  understated what a matched control does, the control having done less in
  the page.

## Findings and their disposition

- **For the next authorized step: the declaration of the two faces.** Find a
  declaration in which a space, and the punctuation and digits the two
  scripts share, are drawn by the face of the text around them, and measure
  it with this suite against the present one. It is a change to approved
  typography (DEC-142), so it is the owner's to approve; no font is removed
  by it. If the pages stop accumulating, the cause is shown and removed at
  once. If they do not, the next question is the frames over 50 ms.
- **A longer run is still needed, after the corrections and not before.**
  This run shows what grows and where, not that it stops. The comparable
  one-hour run DEC-143 asks for should use this suite's matched control and
  fixed cycles, with a diagnostic pass (`--diagnostic`) that collects before
  every dump.
- **The release gate is not met and is not waived by this work.**
- **Left as measured.** The browser's record for every table it styles
  (0.54 MiB in 310 cycles, in every case) is the browser's and the page's.
  JavaScript in use rose by 0.2 MiB in 310 cycles with the extension; to be
  read again in the hour.

No wide-audit trigger fired: the change is to the measuring script only.
