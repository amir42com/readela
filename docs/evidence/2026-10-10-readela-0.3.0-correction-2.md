## The failed return on ChatGPT: cause

Reported by the owner: in a long ChatGPT conversation, Return found a place
below the reader and did not find one far above.

Cause, from the observations above and from the code at `8fe6b63`:

1. **The scrolling region counts from its end.** The search at `8fe6b63`
   moved through the conversation by assigning positive values to
   `scrollTop`, from 0 to the scrollable range. In a region laid out with
   `column-reverse` every positive value is clamped to 0, which is the end of
   the conversation. Each of the 40 stops therefore looked at the same place,
   the end, and the search ended as not found. A place near the end was found
   only because it was already in the document or came into it there. Saving
   had the same fault in a smaller form: the position kept with a place
   (`p`) was computed from the same coordinate.
2. **The earlier part is not there to be searched.** A conversation opens
   with its last few turns. What came before is loaded a piece at a time,
   each time the beginning of what is loaded is shown. A search that visits
   evenly spaced positions of the range it measured at the start never
   reaches text the site has not loaded yet.

The limit of 40 stops and 12 seconds was not the cause: with the first fault
no number of stops moves the view.

## What was corrected

### Return

- **One coordinate for both layouts.** A position is now the distance from
  the beginning of the conversation, whichever way the region counts
  (`scrolling()` in `src/page/reader.js`). A region is taken to count from its
  end when its `scrollTop` is negative or its computed `flex-direction` is
  `column-reverse`. Reading a position, moving to one and moving by a
  distance go through it. The position kept with a place uses it too; a
  place saved by the build of `8fe6b63` on ChatGPT has a meaningless `p`,
  which only costs the search its first guess of a direction.
- **A walk, not a grid.** After at most ten jumps by row number where the
  site numbers its rows (Claude, unchanged in kind), the search walks in the
  direction the place is expected in and then in the other, each from where
  the reader was. One stretch ends at the edge of the unbroken run of turns
  in the document on that side, so no part of the conversation is passed
  without having been in the document; where no turn is in the document it
  is four fifths of a screen.
- **Waiting for the site at the beginning.** When a walk towards the
  beginning reaches the beginning of what is loaded, the search waits up to
  2.5 seconds for the scrollable range to grow, and goes on if it does.
- **Limits: 90 stops and 30 seconds**, from 40 and 12. Reason: a return to a
  place far above now takes one wait for the site per piece of earlier
  conversation, which the grid never did. On the synthetic long conversation
  (40 turns, loaded five at a time after 250 ms, the place in the seventh
  turn and the reader at the end, six loads) a return took about 10.6
  seconds in Chrome and 10.1 in Firefox. The limits are **assumed** to be
  enough for conversations of the order of a hundred turns on the real site;
  the time the real site takes for one piece was not measured. What the site
  loaded stays loaded, so a second Return continues from further up.
- **Unchanged:** a place is trusted only inside the response it was saved in;
  an ambiguous or changed place is not returned to; the search stops at once
  on wheel, touch, pointer or key input, on a change of conversation and when
  Readela is turned off; unless the place was found or the reader took over,
  the conversation is put back where it was (by its distance from the nearer
  end, since the range may have grown); a place not found stays saved.

### Saving and arriving clear of the header

- **Cause.** At `8fe6b63` the block saved without a selection was the first
  readable block *intersecting* the top of the reading area, and a block was
  taken as visible if a hit test found it. On ChatGPT the block at the top is
  usually one whose beginning has scrolled out under the header, so the
  paragraph saved was one the reader saw only the end of. On Claude the
  header lies over the text and lets the pointer through, so the hit test
  reported text under the header as visible.
- **The reading area** is now the scrolling region less whatever lies over
  its top and bottom edges, found from geometry: a positioned element (fixed,
  sticky, or absolute outside the scrolled content) within 8px of the edge,
  at least 30% of the region's width and at most 40% of its height, whether
  or not it takes pointer input. No header height is written into the code.
- **Saving without a selection** takes the first readable block of a response
  that *begins* at least 8px inside that area. Only when no block begins in
  view (one paragraph fills the screen) is it the block at the top. A
  selection in view still says exactly which block.
- **Arriving.** Return places the block's beginning a fifth of the reading
  area (at most 120px) below the top of the area, or at the top of the area
  when the block is taller than the room. It then looks the place up again
  and requires it to begin inside the area before the emphasis is shown and
  before "Returned to your saved place" is reported; where the page moved it
  in the meantime, the placement is corrected, up to three times.

### Inline code and wide tables

- **Inline code, keyboard and sample text** under Paper and Night: a text
  colour of their own (`#14594E` on Paper, `#8FD6C4` on Night; contrast with
  the chip's ground 6.3:1 and 8.1:1), the chip's ground, and a one-pixel
  line (`#C4B89E`, `#5E574D`) drawn as an inset shadow so that nothing
  changes size. The site's border colour is made transparent, so no colour
  of the site's theme is left on the chip. Complete code blocks stay the
  site's, as before.
- **Wide tables.** A table wrapper wider than the text was painted with the
  surface colour at its own width, which left square strips of that colour
  beyond the rounded text surface, and inner wrappers painted again. Now the
  outermost wrapper carries the surface's ground with a margin of 0.625rem to
  each side and rounded corners, drawn as a shadow, and wrappers inside it
  paint nothing. No width, margin or overflow of the site's is changed; the
  table scrolls sideways as it did, and nothing is clipped.

### Popup: status and count

Beside the Saved place heading: `Saved here · 7 total` or
`Not saved here · 7 total`. The first part is the page's answer for this
conversation; the count is the number of valid places in the stored object,
read by the popup and updated when that object changes, which is after the
browser has stored a save or a clear. On a page with no conversation the
count stands alone. At the limit, Save place answers "All 1000 saved places
are in use. Clear one to save this conversation."

### Bookmark in the site's list

The link of a conversation that has a place gets `data-readela-saved`, and
the stylesheet draws a 6 by 11 pixel filled bookmark in Readela's accent at
the start of the row as the link's `::after`: absolutely positioned, with no
pointer input, so the row's text, layout and behaviour are untouched. Which
rows: the adapter's `rows` hook; the path of each link's `href` gives the
conversation identifier, the same function that reads the page's own address
turns it into the key, and the key is looked up among the stored places. A
conversation under a project route and under its direct route is one
conversation. Rows are looked at when a place is saved or cleared, when the
stored places change (also from another tab) and once a second with the
existing address check; no observer was added for the sidebar. A link whose
offset parent is not its row is left alone. Off removes every mark.

### Capacity

`MARK_LIMIT` is 1000, from 100. At `8fe6b63` the list was cut to its newest
100 on every write, so the 101st save silently dropped the oldest place. Now
a save for a conversation without a place is refused at the limit (`canSave`)
and nothing is dropped; a conversation with a place can update or clear it at
the limit. The stored format and its version (2) are unchanged. A place is
at most about 190 bytes as stored, so a thousand are under 200,000 bytes;
each save rewrites the object.

### Readela Sans

- Latin and Latin Extended text is shown in **Inter**, Persian and Arabic
  script in **Vazirmatn**, by three `@font-face` rules limited by
  `unicode-range`. The visible choices are still exactly `Original` and
  `Readela Sans`.
- Inter is packaged as the official variable pair, upright and italic, so
  italic and every weight are the font's own and nothing is synthesised. The
  alternative considered, four static files (regular, italic, bold, bold
  italic), is about 459,000 bytes against 740,216, and would synthesise or
  drop the weights between and beyond them, which both sites use (500 and
  600). Size was accepted for fidelity; the owner may prefer the other.
- `pre`, `code`, `kbd`, `samp`, mathematics and SVG keep their fonts, as
  before. A unit the site presents as a whole inside reading text (a capsule)
  inherited Readela Sans in the first attempt; it is now given the site's own
  font back, which the reader measures on the conversation region and keeps
  in one custom property on the root element while Readela Sans is on.

### Saving from the keyboard

**Alt+Shift+S**, read in the content script as the physical key (`KeyS`) with
Alt and Shift and without Control or Meta, so it is the same key in a Persian
layout. Why this combination: neither site sets an `accesskey`; the
shortcuts the sites advertise use Control; Alt+S alone opens a menu in
Firefox on Windows; and neither browser lists Alt+Shift+S among its own
shortcuts. It calls the same function as the popup's button. It is ignored
when the event's target or the focused element is in a form, a text area, an
editable element or a text box, on key repeat, during composition, and on a
page with no conversation; it exists only while Readela is on. The saved
paragraph shows the brief emphasis once the browser has stored the place
(no animation under reduced motion). Nothing is shown when the place could
not be saved. No `commands` entry, permission or background script was
added. Return and Clear have no key.

## Privacy and trust effect

- **Stored:** nothing new. The same two objects; a place is the same ten
  values, fingerprints and small numbers. No title, text, address or site
  identifier of a conversation is stored, for the bookmark or for anything
  else. Fingerprints are local matching metadata, not encryption and not
  anonymisation.
- **Read:** newly, the path of the conversation links in the site's own
  list, in memory, and key presses on a conversation page for one
  combination.
- **Written to the page:** newly, one attribute on the links of saved
  conversations (the application shell was untouched before), and one custom
  property on the root element while Readela Sans is on.
- **Manifest:** two more web-accessible font files for the two supported
  hosts. Permissions (`storage`), match patterns and the absence of a
  background script are unchanged.
- **Scrolling:** a return can now make ChatGPT load the earlier part of a
  conversation, as the reader's own scrolling to its beginning does.
- **Network:** none, as before.
